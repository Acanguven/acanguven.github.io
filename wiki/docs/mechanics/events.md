# Events & random encounters

**Life Simulator Reloaded** runs on **situations that fire as you play**: where you are, what you own, your job, and optional story packs all feed into what can happen next. What feels “random” is usually **weighted chance** plus **hidden prerequisites** (skills, story progress, how far along a relationship is).

## How events trigger

Common moments when something new can happen:

- **Day pass** — start or end of a simulated day; good for weather, news, maintenance, and ambient life.
- **Location change** — arriving somewhere can spawn encounters (school, street, workplace).
- **After specific actions** — finishing a job shift, using an item, or resolving a choice.
- **Story packs** — crime, celebrity, and monster content add their own encounters on top of everyday life.

Events can show as **logs with popups**, **choice dialogs**, or **quiet changes to your stats**—always read the text before tapping through.

:::tip
If an event “never happens,” you are usually missing a **location**, **minimum stat**, or **story step**. Progress the main loop a few days elsewhere, then return.
:::

## Random events during day pass

Day-pass events simulate **life noise**: small wins, small losses, social pings, illness teases, opportunity knocks. They keep long runs from feeling sterile.

**Handling them:**

- **Stable meters first** — low health, energy, or morale skew outcomes toward harsher branches.
- **Save gems** for branches that threaten **jail time**, **major stat loss**, or **quest failure** if you play hardcore.
- **Note recurring patterns** — some chains repeat daily until you complete a milestone.

## School events

School content clusters around **education**, **social growth**, and **early career seeds**. Expect truancy vs. study tradeoffs, bully or friend arcs, and skills that pay off much later in your character’s life.

## Prison events

While **in prison**, events lean on **relationships inside jail**, **prison reputation**, and **fights**. Outcomes can reduce sentence, start brawls, or offer risky choices. Read **risk** carefully: a short temper can extend your stay.

## Monster encounters

Monster storyline content adds **combat-style** and **exploration** encounters—often tied to specific places, gear (trackers, weapons), or quest stages. Preparation beats rushing in blind: gear, skills, and escape options matter.

## General playbook

| Situation | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| **Unclear risk** | Choose the option that protects **meters** first, money second. |
| **Skill check shown** | Train the skill or use a **boost or gem** if the prize is unique. |
| **Repeat failure** | Change **location**, **job**, or **relationship** context—events often key off where you are in life. |

:::tip
Some **story branches block each other**: if something feels “locked out,” you may have already gone down a path that rules out the other outcome earlier in the run.
:::
