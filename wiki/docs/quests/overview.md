# Quests overview

In **Life Simulator Reloaded**, **quests** are multi-step goals tracked in your quest log. Each quest is made of **milestones** you complete one at a time. Finishing every milestone completes the quest and grants the **reward** (usually money, items, or both).

## How milestones progress

- Milestones unlock when you perform the matching action (buy an item, win a fight, pass a day in prison, and so on).
- Some milestones can complete out of order if you already meet the condition when the quest starts.
- Completed quests stay completed; you cannot repeat them on the same character.

## When quests appear

- **Tutorial line** — Core survival and quality-of-life quests often start automatically as you age into young adulthood (typically **ages 18–23**), guiding market use, jobs, housing, and social goals.
- **Job and module quests** — Starting certain jobs (for example beggar) or entering key locations (Hunter's Guild, prison) can auto-start related quests.
- **Childhood** — One early quest begins around **age 4** and must be finished before age 10.

## Rewards

Rewards are granted **once** when the final milestone completes. Typical payouts:

- **Cash** — scales with difficulty (tutorials → hundreds, master hunter → thousands).
- **Items** — consumables, gear, or crafting mats tied to the quest theme.
- **Indirect perks** — some quests unlock **achievements** or gate **locations** (for example tracker tiers).

If you complete milestones but see no reward, verify the **entire** quest shows as complete; partial progress does not pay out.

## Tips

- Open the **quest screen** regularly so you know what to do next; rewards often fund the next stage of progression.
- If a milestone seems stuck, check **location**, **inventory**, and **job level** requirements—many quests assume you are in the right place with the right gear.
- **Difficulty**: tutorial quests are **easy**; long grinds (100 begs, 500 monster kills) are **medium–hard** mainly due to time.
- **Modules**: Crime, Celebrity, and Monster Hunter add their own quest lines on top of **core** content—buy or enable those modules if a wiki page references them and you do not see the quest.
