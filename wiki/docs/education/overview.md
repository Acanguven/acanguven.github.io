# Education overview

**Education** is how you raise **skill ceilings** faster than job experience alone. In **Life Simulator Reloaded**, courses cost **money**, take **in-game days**, and apply **daily skill gain** toward a **max level** cap for that enrollment.

## Course categories

| Category | What it represents | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **School** | Childhood fundamentals | Boost **academics** and school success early |
| **Apprenticeship** | Trade-style intro programs | First rung for bartender, cook, developer, acting, economics |
| **College** | Advanced programs | Unlocks senior job ranks; often **requires** the apprenticeship twin |
| **Specialist** | Focused electives | Deep dives: driving, self-defense, persuasion workshops, gourmet cooking |

:::tip
Read listed **requirements** like a tech tree: if a college course names another course as prerequisite, finish that course **before** you burn days expecting progress.
:::

## How education affects careers

- **Hard gates:** Some promotions require finishing a specific **course**—no certificate, no title.
- **Soft power:** Even without a hard gate, higher skills pass **decisions** and **interviews** that gate pay bumps.
- **Calendar cost:** Each active course consumes days; stack courses **sequentially**, not in parallel, unless the game lets you focus on more than one at a time (usually one at a time).

## Cost / benefit framing

- **Cheap short courses** (school, beggar survival) buy **optionality** early.
- **Mid-price apprenticeships** (cook, bartender mixology) pay back quickly if that job is your income core.
- **Expensive specialist/college** tracks (gourmet, secure driving, professional acting) are **long-run investments**—fund them with delivery or mid-tier service work.

:::tip
You can **accelerate** some education spends with **gems** when the game offers it—save gems for the longest programs if you are impatient, not for courses you can finish in a week of play.
:::

## Suggested mindset

Pick **one** economic vertical (food, tech, finance, fame) and align **every** course purchase to that vertical for the first in-game year. Generalists are fun but slower to hit the flashy promotions.
