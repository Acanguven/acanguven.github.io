# Cook

The **cook** (culinary) track is the **back-of-house** restaurant career: you start in **dishwashing** and **cleaning-heavy** roles, then climb toward **line cook**, **pastry**, and executive-style titles as **cooking** and related skills climb.

## Key skills

- **Cooking** — the star of the ladder; promotions and better plates depend on it.
- **Cleaning** matters heavily at the bottom ranks (dish pit reality).
- **Leadership** and **mixology** can appear at fancier tiers where the kitchen interfaces with service or specials.

:::tip
If you hate the dish pit, rush the **basic cooking apprenticeship** early. It is inexpensive and moves you toward levels that actually train **cooking** every day instead of only **cleaning**.
:::

## Work location

You are tied to the **restaurant** flow: expect **questions** and **decisions** about ingredients, timing, and pressure during service. Good answers convert to morale, skill bumps, or small bonuses; bad ones tax energy and mood.

## Education

Pair the job with **cooking courses**: a short **apprenticeship-style** course unlocks fundamentals, while **gourmet** or **masterclass** tracks raise the ceiling for late promotions. Specialist-tier cooking programs are pricey—save during mid ranks.

## Strategy

1. Take **cook** when you can tolerate **energy swings**; rush hours hurt if you are already depleted.
2. Alternate **work** with **rest** or cheap morale fixes until cooking income stabilizes.
3. Plan one **big** specialist course before you aim at celebrity-chef fantasy levels.

:::tip
Restaurant careers synergize with **waitressing** if you want to see both sides of the house—but juggling two jobs is slower than finishing one ladder first.
:::
