# Prison jobs

When you are **in prison**, conventional careers pause. Two institutional jobs remain: **laundry** and **barber** (hairdressing). Both train practical skills, pay **modest** wages, and help you survive incarceration with something to do each day.

## Laundry

- **Role fantasy:** washing, folding, keeping linens moving.
- **Skill:** **cleaning** rises quickly from daily passes (variable gains).
- **Levels:** amateur and regular washer tiers; higher tier expects strong **cleaning** already.

:::tip
If you expect a **long sentence**, laundry is the fastest way to rocket **cleaning** for post-release jobs like kitchen dish pit—think of it as involuntary training.
:::

## Barber

- **Role fantasy:** cutting hair inside the yard or shop.
- **Skill:** **haircutting** (barber skill) is the focus; **master** tier also expects **cleaning** because hygiene rules the workshop.
- **Levels:** apprentice, normal, and master—each step raises pay slightly.

Barber promotions need **haircutting** thresholds (50 for normal, 100 for master) plus **cleaning** for the final jump.

## Interviews

Neither prison job uses **interview** flows—you are assigned work by circumstance, not HR.

## Strategy

- Pick **laundry** if you want **one** stat mastered fast; pick **barber** if you want a **craft** that pays marginally better at the top.
- Plan post-prison employment: **haircutting** supports civilian barber fantasies; **cleaning** supports cook or service roles.
- Stay out of **fights** when possible; injuries waste days you could spend grinding these skills.

:::tip
Treat prison jobs as **stat bootcamps**, not wealth engines. Your real payday returns when you walk free with a maxed utility skill.
:::
