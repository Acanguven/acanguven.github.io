# School jobs

**School jobs** are early-career roles aimed at ages roughly **16–22**: low base pay, quick interviews, and steady **skill drip** while you study or explore the city. They are ideal for **first money**, **sports** or **communication** training, and learning the promotion loop without harsh gates.

## Available roles

| Job | What you do | Skills emphasized |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Babysitter** | Watch neighborhood kids | Calmness, communication |
| **Dog walker** | Walk pets | Sports |
| **Tutor** | Help younger students | Academics, communication |
| **Lawn mower** | Yard work | Sports |
| **Newspaper delivery** | Route delivery | Sports |
| **Grocery bagger** | Checkout support | Communication |

Each job has **beginner** and **experienced** tiers. Experienced levels ask for **higher skills** (for example sports or communication thresholds) but pay a few units more per day.

:::tip
**Tutor** is the odd one out: it expects **academics** early. If you skipped school stats, start with **dog walker**, **lawn mower**, or **bagger** to fund courses or raise communication first.
:::

## Pay and perks

Payments are **small** compared to mid-game careers—think of them as **allowance plus training**. Some roles can still trigger **occasional tips** or morale events, which helps a broke teen recover from bad days.

## Strategy

1. Line up **one** school job while you complete **school courses** or raise **academics** if you plan a knowledge-heavy life.
2. Use interviews to learn the **question patterns**; failures are cheap at this stage.
3. Resign cleanly when you outgrow the pay band—bank **skills**, not nostalgia.

:::tip
If you want **sports** for later acrobat, delivery, or combat-adjacent content, **dog walker** + **newspaper delivery** stack sports gains efficiently while paying you something back.
:::
