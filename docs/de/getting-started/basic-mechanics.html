Basic Mechanics

Life Simulator Reloaded runs on a simple loop: choose where to be, do things that cost time and resources, survive the day, and come back tomorrow with new events and opportunities. Here is how that loop fits together.

The day pass​

A “day” is the main unit of progress. When you end a day (or the game advances time):

  • Your character ages along the calendar (20 in-game days = 1 in-game year).
  • Daily hooks fire across items, locations, quests, and modules — this is where diseases heal, rent comes due, prison sentences tick, and story beats trigger.
Think in days, plan in years

Short-term: “Can I afford food and sleep today?” Long-term: “Where do I want my skills and relationships by next year?”

Locations, actions, and events​

PieceRole
LocationsPlaces you travel to — home, work hubs, shops, special areas (hospital, police station, etc.). Where you stand decides which actions appear.
ActionsThings you do at a location: work shifts, study, socialize, commit crimes, use items — often tied to skills, money, and risk.
EventsQuestions, decisions, and random encounters that pop up as you play. Some are harmless flavor; others reshape money, relationships, or legal status.

You will also meet NPCs, build relationships, buy and use items, take courses, and apply for jobs — all through this location + action lens.

Health, energy, and morale​

Three vital statuses track whether you can keep going:

StatusStarting currentMax
Health75100
Energy75100
Morale75100

Death​

If any one of health, energy, or morale hits 0, you die. Treat all three as hard loss conditions.

Danger zone​

When any of these stats falls to 20 or below, you are in danger — the game treats this as critically low. Expect worse outcomes from risky actions and prioritize recovery (rest, food, medical help, morale fixes).

Zero is game over

Do not tank one bar to “save” another. A dead genius is still dead.

Mini-games​

Many actions resolve through mini-games — short skill checks (timing, memory, reflex, etc.) that stand in for pickpocketing, lockpicking, fights, interviews, and more.

  • Success usually grants the full reward; failure may waste time, money, or stats.
  • Practice matters: higher relevant skills and smart prep (items, location, rest) tilt odds in your favor.

See Mini-games for a full catalog and tips.

Gems (premium currency)​

Gems are the premium currency. Typical uses include:

  • Skipping or softening difficult mini-games.
  • Boosting tough decisions when failure is expensive.
  • Accelerating certain time-gated or grindy paths (education, recovery, etc., depending on content).
Spend gems like a lifeline, not confetti

Save them for high-stakes checks — promotions you have waited for, prison survival, rare quest branches — not every minor failure.

How this connects to modules​

Core supplies the baseline loop above. Crime, Celebrity, and Monster Hunter add locations, actions, items, and quests that plug into the same day pass and stat rules — with extra risk/reward on top.
